## What Is Retransfer Card Printing?

Retransfer printing is a two-stage printing process used in professional ID card production.

First, the printer creates the card image on a specialised transfer film. The film is then bonded to the card surface using heat and pressure.

This differs from direct-to-card printing, where the image is printed directly onto the card.

### How retransfer printing works

1. **Card preparation:** The blank card is fed into the printer.
2. **Image printing:** The printer transfers the image onto a special film.
3. **Image transfer:** The printed film is applied to the card surface.
4. **Finished card:** The card receives the personalised image across the printable surface.

The process is useful when an organisation needs detailed designs, edge-to-edge printing or compatibility with supported card constructions that may be less suitable for direct printing.

### Benefits of retransfer technology

#### Edge-to-edge card designs

Retransfer technology can print across the card surface without the conventional unprinted border often associated with direct-to-card printing.

#### Fine text and detailed graphics

A suitable high-resolution model can reproduce small text, detailed logos and intricate visual elements for professional identification applications.

#### Flexible card applications

Retransfer printing can be suitable for supported PVC, polycarbonate and other compatible card materials. Always verify the specific card construction against the manufacturer's approved media specifications.

#### Potential advantages for smart cards

Retransfer printing can be useful for certain cards with embedded electronics or uneven surfaces. However, compatibility must be confirmed for the card type and printer configuration.

#### Additional security options

Depending on the model and installed options, organisations can evaluate UV printing, encoding, laminating and other card security features.

## Retransfer Printers vs Direct-to-Card Printers

Brands such as Evolis, Entrust and HID Fargo offer direct-to-card and/or retransfer solutions across their respective product portfolios. The correct comparison is between specific models, not brand names alone.

### Comparison

| Comparison factor | Retransfer printing | Direct-to-card printing |
|---|---|---|
| Printing method | Image printed onto film and transferred to card | Image printed directly onto card |
| Edge-to-edge designs | Suitable for full-surface designs | May leave a small unprinted border, depending on model |
| Image detail | High-resolution options available | High-resolution options also available |
| Card surface compatibility | Useful for selected supported card constructions | Works well with suitable compatible cards |
| Production cost | Transfer film adds to consumable requirements | Often fewer consumable stages |
| Typical selection priority | Print finish, card compatibility and detail | Cost-effective everyday card printing |
| Best application | Professional, detailed or specialised card programmes | General identification and routine card issuance |

Retransfer is not automatically better for every customer. Direct-to-card printers can be more economical for straightforward card programmes, while retransfer may be preferable when full-surface printing, particular card materials or specialised card designs are required.
